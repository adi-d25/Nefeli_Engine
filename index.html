01 / The problem

Production data tells you what happened. It rarely tells you how the whole line behaved.

Machine counters, fault codes and shift records capture fragments of a production process. But those fragments are difficult to connect into a reliable picture of flow across stations, buffers and shared resources. Nefeli Engine is exploring a more useful way to bring that picture together.

01 / Fragmented signals

Data without context

Machine events and production records live in different systems. Understanding how one station affects another takes time and manual investigation.

02 / Static documentation

A model that falls behind

Production lines change. Equipment is replaced, buffers are added and processes are adjusted. Documentation and simulations can quickly stop reflecting the floor.

03 / Limited visibility

Symptoms without the full picture

A counter can record a reject or a stop without showing the sequence of events around it, or how that event affected the rest of the line.

02 / The approach

From operational logs to a model you can question.

The starting point is not a perfect factory blueprint. It is the information a line already produces. The aim is to turn that information into a model that can be checked against observed behaviour, improved over time and used to explore practical decisions.

STEP 01

Bring in the data

Start with available machine logs, counters, event records and production data. Work with what a plant can provide.

STEP 02

Describe line behaviour

Estimate how stations operate, including cycle times, downtime, queues and the relationships between stages.

STEP 03

Check against reality

Compare model outputs with observed production. Identify where assumptions no longer match the way the line is running.

STEP 04

Explore what could change

Test scenarios such as a different buffer size, a station running slower, or a change in production demand.

03 / Product direction

Start with one line. Earn the right to model more.

Manufacturing environments are different, and a useful model has to account for the equipment, process and data available in each one. The product direction is deliberately incremental: prove that a small model can stay close to observed behaviour before expanding its scope.

Initial focus

One production line, understood in detail

Build and validate a model of a defined process. Make throughput, constraints, downtime and changes in behaviour easier to inspect and reason about.

Longer-term direction

Connected models across a plant

Bring validated line models together to understand interactions across production areas, material flow and shared resources, without treating an entire factory as one monolithic system.
